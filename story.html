The story of Gromit Forger

Well spotted. Nice work, Agent. You found the hidden page.
Gromit Forger is the alter ego of Henry, who designed this site for his graphic design students at SCAD. They come from the Spider-Verse, a universe where Henry never says a word. Gromit Forger doesn’t exist, but nothing about them is an accident. Here is why.

Why a fictional designer

A real student’s site would be about one person’s work. A fictional designer lets everyone study the structure, the writing, and the decisions without copying anyone.

The layout follows the discipline of the printed page, the same principles behind Intent Collective. Twelve columns, running text six columns wide, seven type sizes, and spacing in steps of four.

Why Gromit Forger

Gromit

Gromit is the dog from Nick Park’s Wallace & Gromit films. He never says a word. He reads, bakes, builds, and quietly saves his inventor friend Wallace from every disaster Wallace creates. A designer who lets the work do the talking could not have a better namesake.

Forger

The Forgers are the family in Tatsuya Endo’s Spy × Family. Loid is a spy, Yor is an assassin, Anya can read minds, and Bond the dog can see the future. None of them knows the others’ secrets, yet together they solve almost any problem, with warmhearted empathy. Yor fights like crazy for her family. Those are the qualities every design team hopes to find in a new hire.

The projects

Top Bun

The bakery Wallace and Gromit run in A Matter of Loaf and Death (2008). Gromit Forger has the early shifts on their resume too.

Operation Strix

Loid Forger’s mission in Spy × Family. He builds a fake family to get close to his target through a school. A book with a second text hidden in its margins is that double life, printed.

The Wrong Trousers

The 1993 Wallace & Gromit film that ends with Gromit on a speeding model train, laying new track in front of it piece by piece. A title sequence where the type lays its own track is the same chase.

Stella Stars

Eden Academy, Anya’s school, rewards students with Stella stars and punishes them with Tonitrus bolts. Eight stars make an Imperial Scholar, and eight bolts mean expulsion. It is a real system of rules, ready to be visualized.

Cracking Cheese

“Cracking” is Wallace’s favorite word, and cheese is his favorite food. In A Grand Day Out (1989) he builds a rocket to the moon because he believes it is made of cheese.

Wanted

Feathers McGraw, the silent penguin thief from The Wrong Trousers, who returns in Vengeance Most Fowl (2024). He hides in plain sight with a rubber glove on his head, passing as a chicken. An app that helps a whole village report where he was last seen felt just right.

Bondman

The cartoon spy hero Anya adores in Spy × Family. A magazine for his youngest fans had to work for readers who can’t read well yet.

Anti-Pesto

Wallace and Gromit’s humane pest control business in The Curse of the Were-Rabbit (2005), hired to guard a village’s prize vegetables before the Giant Vegetable Competition.

Thorn Princess

Yor Forger’s secret name. By day she is a gentle clerk at City Hall. Letters that look elegant from across a City Hall lobby and turn sharp up close are her portrait.

Other things to find

  • Bond, the family dog, “always seems to know where we’re going before I do.”
  • The Golden Carrot on their resume is the trophy of the Giant Vegetable Competition.
  • Their Stella Star comes from a volunteer day at Eden Academy.
  • The Wensleydale Type Club is named after Wallace’s favorite cheese.
  • They build model railways, sometimes at speed.
  • The line at the foot of their resume, “I don’t say much. The work does.”
